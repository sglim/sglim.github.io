Early this year I started reading Hacker News every day. The plan was English practice. Read real technical writing by native speakers, pick up how they phrase things, get faster. It worked for that. It also did something I did not plan for.

Here is the English part first, because that was the goal.

Reading the comments is better practice than reading the articles. Articles are edited. Comments are how engineers actually talk. Short sentences, dry jokes, the specific way people disagree politely and then not so politely. I learned more about how to say "I think you are wrong" in English from HN threads than from any class. There are about five accepted ways to do it and they all start with acknowledging the other person had a point.

I also learned that my English was more formal than it needed to be. My emails sounded like a textbook. After a few months of HN I started writing shorter and it read better. Native speakers do not say "I would like to inquire whether". They say "quick question".

Now the part I did not plan.

HN is a firehose of things I did not know I did not know. Not the front page hype, that fades. The comments from someone who ran a database team for a decade explaining why the article is wrong. A thread about how a company handled an outage. Someone's twenty year old blog post that a commenter dug up. I have a list of things I learned this year that I would never have found by searching, because I would not have known what to search for.

Some patterns:

- The best comment is often the second or third, not the top one. The top one is usually the fastest, not the best.
- When a post is about a topic I actually know, the comments are about 30% wrong. I assume that ratio holds for topics I do not know. It keeps me humble about the other 70%.
- The "Ask HN" threads about careers and burnout are the same conversation every few months. That is not a criticism. Some conversations need to happen every few months.

What I regret is not taking notes. I read something good, I think "I should remember that", and a week later it is gone. Starting now I am going to keep a file. Just a line per thing. Link, one sentence, why it mattered. If it turns out to be useless I will stop. I suspect it will not be useless.

If you are a developer who does not read HN, I would recommend it. Not for the news. For the people in the comments who have already made the mistake you are about to make and are willing to tell you about it, for free, in English.
