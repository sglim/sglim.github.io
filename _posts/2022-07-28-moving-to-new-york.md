I am writing this from an apartment in Manhattan with three boxes still unopened and an internet connection that took four days to arrive. We moved to New York.

Same company. Momenti has people on both sides of the Pacific and the option to be on this side was there, and in January I wrote that there was a conversation at home about living somewhere English is the first language. The conversation ended in a yes.

Some first impressions, one week in, with no attempt at insight.

The heat is different. Seoul is humid in July. New York is humid in July and also the subway platforms are ovens and nobody warned me. I have adjusted my definition of "walkable".

Everything requires a Social Security number and you cannot get a Social Security number quickly. Apartment, bank, phone, all of them want a number I do not have yet or a credit history I have never had. I now understand why every immigrant story includes a chapter about paperwork. It is not a metaphor. It is forms.

People are direct. In a good way, mostly. At the bank the person told me my plan was bad and what to do instead, in one sentence, without the softening I am used to. I liked it. I also noticed that I softened my reply out of habit and it made the conversation take twice as long.

My English is fine and also not fine. I can read anything. I can write, clearly. I can talk about work. What I cannot do yet is catch the third sentence when three people are talking at once in a bar. That will come or it will not. Either way it is humbling to be thirty six and be the slow one in the room.

Work is strange in a specific way. The team is split across time zones now with me on the far side. Seoul's morning is my evening. I am going to have to figure out a schedule that is not "always on". I have a time tracker. I will look at the data in a few months and see what I actually did versus what I intended.

Things I am looking forward to:

- Meetups. Real ones, in English, where the people in the room built the things I read about.
- Not being the person who has to explain what the company does in a second language.
- The bagels. Specifically the bagels.

Things I already miss:

- Delivery that arrives in twenty minutes.
- Knowing where everything is.
- My 3D printer, which did not make the trip and is sitting in a box at my parents' place.

I do not know how long we will stay. A few years, probably. Long enough that it counts. I will keep writing here, and I suspect the posts are about to get more personal and less about protobuf. That is fine. That is what this year was supposed to be.
