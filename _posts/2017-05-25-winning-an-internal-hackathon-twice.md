Our company has this thing every quarter. One week, drop your normal work, build whatever you want, demo it on Friday. Everybody votes. There is a small prize and a large amount of bragging.

The first one this year, my team built a receipt scanner. Point the phone at a receipt, it reads the items and prices, you split the bill. Simple idea, not simple to make work in five days with real receipts that are crumpled and printed on thermal paper that fades. We won. The demo worked on the second try, which in demo terms is a clean run.

Then there was a demo day in the second quarter, a bigger version with outside guests. Different team, different idea, a voice thing this time. We won that too.

I want to write down why, because I don't think it was because we were the smartest team in the room. We were not.

We picked something that could fail visibly. A receipt scanner either reads the receipt or it doesn't. The audience knows instantly. Some teams built things that were technically impressive but the demo was a slide with a graph. A graph does not make people clap.

We cut scope on Tuesday. Not Thursday. Tuesday. By the second day we knew which parts were hard and we deleted two features on the spot. The teams that cut on Thursday night demoed something half broken. The teams that never cut demoed a plan.

One person owned the demo. Not the code, the demo. Which phone, which receipt, which order to show things, what to say if it fails. I have watched too many good projects die because three people were talking over each other in front of the projector.

We practiced the failure. We picked one receipt that we knew worked and one that we knew was flaky. We showed the good one first. If the flaky one failed we had a line ready. It did not fail, but knowing we had the line made us calm, and calm demos better.

The prize is nothing. Some money for dinner. The real thing is that for one week, the whole company sees what you can do when nobody is telling you what to do. People remember. When the travel app needs a hand later, the people who saw the receipt demo are the ones who say yes.

Also, I got to be the hardware guy for once. The voice demo needed a stand for the microphone and I printed one on the 3D printer that someone bought for an IoT experiment. First thing I ever printed. It was ugly. It held the mic. I think I might have a new hobby.

Next quarter I'm going to try to not win. Let somebody else have the dinner. We'll see if I can resist.
