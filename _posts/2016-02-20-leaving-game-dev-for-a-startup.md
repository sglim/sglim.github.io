Last week was my last week at the game company. One year almost exactly. Next month I start at a startup that does not have a product yet.

People asked me why. Honestly, the game job was good. I launched a real MMO. I learned more C++ in a year than in the five before it. The team was strong and the stress tests were fun in a sick way.

So here is the actual reason, as much as I can put it into words.

At a game company the game is the point. Engineering serves the game. That is correct and healthy. But it also means that as an engineer you are always downstream. The designers decide, the engine decides, the publisher decides, and you make it work. I got good at making it work. I did not get to decide what "it" was.

The new company is being started by people who came out of the engineering side of a big search company. The pitch was basically: we don't know exactly what we are building yet, but we know how we want to build it. Small team, everybody senior, real code review, real testing, and nobody says "we'll fix it after launch". I'll be the fifth engineer.

I did an internship at that search company years ago. Six months. The code review there changed how I write code. Every line got read by somebody who would ask "why". At the time it was exhausting. Now I miss it. Most places I have worked since, review was a formality. A rubber stamp from someone who was busy. Going back to a place where people actually read the diff is a big part of why I said yes.

The risk is obvious. There is no product. There might never be a product. The salary is fine but the upside is a lottery ticket. I'm turning thirty this year and I don't have kids, and I figured if I'm ever going to bet on a small team it should be now, not at forty with a mortgage.

Things I'm taking with me from games:

- Measure before you optimize, but do actually optimize. Web people talk about performance. Game people ship it.
- A stress test is worth ten design reviews.
- The boring parts, patchers and migrations, are where you earn trust.

Things I'm hoping to leave behind:

- Crunch as a lifestyle.
- Being the last one to hear about a decision that changes my week.

I'll write about the new place once I understand what it is. Right now I don't. That is kind of the point.
