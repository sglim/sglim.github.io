Back in Seoul. Five weeks. The time zone on this post is +0900 again, which felt strange to type.

Reverse culture shock is real and it is petty. Here is a list.

Delivery is instant again and I had forgotten what that does to your planning. In New York you ordered groceries for tomorrow. Here you order at nine and they are outside the door at ten. I have lost the habit of thinking ahead about food and I am not sure I want it back.

The subway has doors on the platform and air conditioning and I stood there on the first day feeling like I was in the future, which is a funny thing to feel about the place you grew up.

People are indirect again. Not worse, just different. I caught myself being too blunt in a conversation with an old friend. Two and a half years of "say the thing" and now I have to remember how to say the thing sideways. I suspect I will land somewhere in between, which is probably where I should have been all along.

Everything is on one app. Banking, taxes, ID, parking. It is convenient and slightly alarming and my American friends would not believe it.

My parents are older than when I left. Obviously. It still landed.

What I am doing with the months before whatever is next:

Not rushing. I said that in 2023 and mostly kept to it, and the job I eventually took was better for the waiting. Same plan. A few conversations, no decisions until spring is over.

Rebuilding the desk. The 3D printer came out of its box at my parents' place. It needs a new nozzle and a lot of dust removed and I am unreasonably happy about that. I printed a calibration cube on the second day. It was a cube.

Looking at what changed in the industry here while I was gone. The AI stuff is everywhere, in every job description, in every conversation with old colleagues. Some of it is hype. Some of it changed how people actually work day to day, in ways that I only half used in New York and need to actually learn now.

Sleeping. Real sleep, in one block, in a time zone where my body and my calendar agree. The first week I woke up at three every morning ready for a standup that did not exist. That has stopped.

What I am not doing: deciding whether the New York years were worth it. They were. I got the English I went for. I worked on a Rust engine and a media format and a data pipeline in a city where the people who wrote the papers were at the next table. I lost some things and one job ended badly. Net positive, clearly, and I do not need to keep doing the math.

Next post will probably be about a job. There are a couple of interesting ones. One is in a field I have never touched and that is exactly what makes it interesting.
