July 5th. A large box. Much larger than the space I had cleared for it, which I should have measured first.

I bought a 3D printer for home. An Ender-5. This is the direct result of the two Ender-3s at the office last year, which I said I would not get addicted to, and then designed a demo stand on, and then printed cable clips on, and then started reading forums about at midnight.

I have had it for five days. Here is the honest state of things.

Assembly took an evening and it was fun in the way that IKEA furniture is fun if you like IKEA furniture. The frame is a cube, the bed moves down as the print goes up, which is different from the Ender-3 where the bed moves back and forth. In theory the cube is more rigid and the prints are cleaner. In practice my first print was a mess and it had nothing to do with the frame.

Bed leveling. Everyone says it. I nodded when people said it. I did not understand it until my first layer came out as spaghetti on one side and a smear on the other. The bed was tilted by less than a millimeter. That is enough. I now own a feeler gauge and a strong opinion about paper thickness.

Second print was a calibration cube. It was a cube. I was disproportionately happy.

Third print was a phone stand from the internet. It worked. It is on my desk. It is slightly ugly and I love it.

What I am learning, five days in:

- The printer is the easy part. The slicer settings are the actual hobby. Layer height, speed, temperature, retraction. Every one of them interacts with every other one.
- Filament absorbs water from the air. In a Korean July that is a real problem. Wet filament pops and leaves little holes. I have a box with silica gel now.
- The room temperature matters. Prints started overnight came out different from prints started in the afternoon. Because the window was open. This is not software.

That last point is the one that stays with me. In my job when something fails I read a log. Here nothing has a log. The failure is physical and the cause is environmental and you learn by looking at the plastic and making a guess. It is humbling. It is also a nice break from a screen.

I am not modeling anything myself yet. Downloading other people's designs and printing them is plenty for now. Someday I'll learn CAD. Probably around the time I need a part that does not exist.

Next upgrade is a better fan duct. Which I will print. On the printer. To improve the printer. This hobby is recursive.
