The company's next round did not close. I am still employed, technically. But work has paused, and I am home in New York in November with a laptop that has nothing urgent on it for the first time in years.

I want to write this now, in the middle of it, rather than later when I know how it turned out.

How it happened, from where I sat: the summer was quiet. Fewer meetings, slower decisions, a Slack that used to be loud at ten pm. I noticed and I did not ask. Then the fall got quieter. Then there was a call. Runway, investors, the market, all the words. Nobody did anything wrong that I could see. The money that was supposed to be there was not there.

What it feels like: not panic. Something flatter. I have a visa tied to this job, an apartment lease, and a routine built around Seoul's morning. All three are now questions. The visa question is the one that keeps me up.

What I am doing about it:

Working anyway. Not for the company, for myself. I have a list of things I wanted to build and never had time for. A crawler for apartment listings, because we might need to move and I want data. A small side project in Flutter that has been in my head since the analyzer work. I open the laptop at the same time every day. The time tracker shows it. The hours are there, the project column is empty, which is a strange thing to see after seven years of every hour having a label.

Talking to people. I have avoided networking for years because I had a job and did not need to. Now I am the person sending the "would love to catch up" message. It is uncomfortable. People have been kinder than I expected. New York has a lot of people who have been through exactly this, and they will tell you about it over coffee without you asking.

Not deciding yet. Every instinct says decide something. Go back to Korea. Take the first offer. Start a company. I am trying to sit with not knowing for a few more weeks, because every decision I have ever made in a panic was worse than the one I made a month later.

Things I am grateful for, because writing them down helps:

- The two and a half years of work I got to do here. The engine, the WASM port, the RFCs, the arrows on frames. None of that goes away because the round did not close.
- The team. We are all in the same boat and nobody has gotten mean about it.
- The time tracking, weirdly. Seven years of data says I work about two thousand hours a year no matter what. It is a comfort to know that the habit is mine and not the job's.

I will write again when something changes. Right now nothing has, except everything.
