I moved here in July 2022. It is November 2024. That is long enough to have opinions and not long enough for them to be right. Here they are anyway.

On work.

I have had two jobs here. One was a Korean company where I was the person on the far side of the ocean, working evenings to overlap with Seoul. The other is a local startup where everyone is in the same room. They taught me opposite things.

The remote job taught me to write. When your team is asleep during your most productive hours, the only way to move things forward is a document they wake up to. Design docs, RFCs, flow diagrams, a written decision instead of a call. I became a better engineer in those two years mostly by becoming a better writer, and I did not notice until I got to a local team and saw how much got decided in hallways and lost by Friday.

The local job taught me to talk. Specifically to disagree out loud, in real time, in English, with people who do it naturally. Korean work culture, or at least the version I grew up in, defers. You raise a concern carefully, once, and if it is not picked up you let it go. Here if you let it go it is gone, and later somebody asks why you did not say anything. I say things now. It took a year and it still costs me more energy than it costs the people around me.

On the city.

It is loud and expensive and I love it in a way that surprises me. Not the postcard parts. The parts where a stranger at a meetup turns out to have written a library I use, and we talk for an hour, and that is a normal Tuesday. The density of people doing interesting things is the actual product of this city and it is worth what it costs.

I lost a 3D printer and a car and gained walking. I walk an hour a day without trying. My feet have opinions about shoes now.

On being a foreigner.

I will always have an accent and I stopped minding this year. What I mind is the third sentence in a loud bar. Still. That may never come. What did come is the ability to run a meeting, push back on a plan, and make a room laugh on purpose, all in a language I learned from textbooks and Hacker News comments. I am proud of that. I do not say that about many things.

The visa situation is stable for now. That sentence has done a lot of work in the last two years.

On what is next.

I do not know. The current job is fine and the product is fun and the team is good. There is also a pull toward home that I did not expect. Parents getting older. Friends having kids. A life in Seoul that continued without me and that I can see from here through a screen. I will not decide anything this year. I said that a year ago too, and then a lot got decided.

Two and a half years. Long enough to count. I am glad I came. I do not know yet whether I am staying. Both of those are true and I am learning to hold them at the same time.
