I exported my time tracking data this week and switched apps. The old one has 5,677 entries in it, from January 12th 2017 to June 18th 2021. Before I put the file in a folder and forget it, here is what four and a half years of tracking my own work looks like.

The totals first. About 2,000 hours a year, every year. 2017 was 2,022. 2018 was 1,906. 2019 was 1,882. 2020 was 1,957. Around 250 to 270 working days each year. I did not plan for the number to be that stable. It just is. Whatever job I had, whatever title, the hours settled in the same place.

Where they went, by year:

2017 was one thing. The travel app, 85 percent. Launch year.

2018 was scattered. The app, then six months of blockchain research, then back to the app, plus a hackathon, plus a demo day. My biggest single category that year was "corp", almost 400 hours of interviews, all hands, committees. That was the year I did 71 hours of interviews.

2019 was the IoT project. Almost 900 hours on one product. Then the consumer app in the fall.

2020 the categories collapse into one word: "CTO". Fourteen hundred hours labeled that. I stopped tracking what I was doing and started tracking that I was doing it. That says something about the job.

Meetings, all years, hover between 12 and 17 percent. Never lower. The year I was CTO it was not higher than the year I was an engineer, which surprised me.

Weekends are almost empty. Under 500 hours total across all four and a half years. I am glad about that one.

The start times are interesting. Most days I start at ten or eleven. There is a bump at one in the afternoon, after lunch. There is a long tail into the evening that got longer in 2020. There are almost nine hundred entries that start at midnight exactly, which are not midnight work sessions, they are entries where I logged a whole day and did not bother with the time. I should have bothered.

The notes are the part I did not expect to value. Half the entries have a note. "Test flight." "Burger party." "About segmentation, with M." "Happy hour." A link to an article about machine learning myths. A link to a Seth Godin post about not shaving the yak. Somebody's name with "1:1" next to it. They are useless as data and they are the only part that brings the day back. I can read "2018-04-26 Burger Party" and remember the room.

What I learned from the whole exercise:

- The hours do not change much. The labels do. Track the labels.
- Write the note. Always write the note.
- Do not make a category called "Working". It is not a category, it is giving up.

The new app is different. It watches which application is in front and files the time automatically. Less honest in one way, since I am not deciding what to call the block. More honest in another, since it knows exactly how long I was in Slack. I will report back.
