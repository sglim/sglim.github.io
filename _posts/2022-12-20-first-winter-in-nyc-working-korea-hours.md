Five months in New York. It is cold in a dry, wind between buildings way that Seoul does not do. And I said in July that I would look at my time tracking data to see what schedule I actually ended up with. I looked. It is not what I intended.

The app I use logs which window is in front, all day, automatically. So this is not what I think I did. It is what the laptop saw.

Before the move, in Seoul, my day was a normal shape. Start around ten, peak between one and six in the afternoon, tail off by eight. One hump.

After the move, two humps.

The first hump is New York daytime. Ten in the morning to around one in the afternoon is the busiest stretch, then it tapers through the afternoon. That is my own work. Engine, player, the NFT thing, docs. Nobody else is awake, so nobody interrupts, so it is the most productive part of the day by a wide margin.

The second hump starts at nine at night and runs to midnight, sometimes one. That is Seoul's morning. Standups, design reviews, the conversations that need three people. Slack is the top app in that window by a mile. In the Seoul data Slack barely registered. Here it is the third most used app of the year.

Between the humps, from about two to eight in the evening, the laptop is mostly closed. I go outside. I cook. I have, for the first time in years, a gap in the middle of the day that is actually mine.

Whether this is good depends on the day. On a good day it is the best schedule I have ever had. Deep work in the morning with no meetings, a real afternoon, a focused evening of collaboration, done by midnight. On a bad day the evening runs long, I am tired for the morning hump, and the afternoon gap gets eaten by naps. The data shows both kinds of days. More good than bad, which surprised me.

Things I have changed after seeing the numbers:

- Meetings only in the evening window. I stopped taking calls in my morning. Seoul adjusted within a couple of weeks. Nobody is as attached to your calendar as you think.
- Hard stop at midnight. The tracker shows a few nights that went to two. Every one of them shows up as a weak next morning. Not worth it.
- Lunch is at two. Because that is when the first hump ends. Fighting that was pointless.

What the data does not show is the loneliness of the second hump. Working at eleven at night while the city outside is doing something else. It is fine. It is also the part I would change first if I could.

Total hours per month are about where they were in Seoul. The shape moved. The amount did not. I keep finding that in this data. The number is stubborn. The labels move around it.

First winter. Radiator that clanks. Bagels on Saturday. Standup at ten pm. It is a strange life and I am mostly enjoying it.
