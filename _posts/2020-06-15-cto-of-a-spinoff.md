Two months into the new job. Except it is not really a new job. It is the same product I started in 2016, at the company it was spun out into two years ago, and my title is now CTO.

I want to be honest about what that title means at this size, because I had a picture in my head and the picture was wrong.

What I thought: strategy, architecture, big decisions, occasional code.

What it is: a lot of people. There are more than twenty engineers across two products, the flight app I know and a deals app I am learning. My calendar is one on ones, planning, and a standing meeting with marketing, sales, and customer support that I did not know I needed until the first one, when I found out CS had been working around a bug for a month because nobody told engineering.

Some things I have learned in eight weeks.

Estimating is now my job, not a thing I do. When sales asks "can we have hotels by fall", the answer shapes hiring and budget. I can no longer say "probably" and figure it out. I have a year of my own time tracking and a multiplier of 1.7, and I am applying it to twenty people who have their own multipliers that I do not know yet.

Design docs are how I stay technical. I cannot review every change anymore. I tried for two weeks and became the bottleneck. What I can do is read every design doc and write some myself. The doc is where the architecture actually lives. The code follows it or the code is wrong.

The hardest thing is not the technology. It is a person who is good at their work being on the wrong project. Moving them is right for the company and feels bad for both of us on the day. I have done it twice already. I did not sleep well either time.

And COVID. We sell flights. I do not need to explain further. The whole industry stopped in March and we are building through it, which is a strange feeling. Half of me thinks this is the best time to build, because when travel comes back we will be ready. The other half looks at the booking graph and goes quiet.

Things I am keeping from being an engineer:

- I still write code every week. Small things. A script, a fix, a prototype. If I stop I will lose the ability to smell a bad design doc.
- I still read the diffs for the parts I designed. Not to approve, just to know.

Things I am learning to let go of:

- Being the one who knows. Twenty people know more than I do about their piece. My job is to make sure the pieces fit.
- Being right. I was overruled on a technical call last week by two senior engineers with a better argument. Good. That is the team working.

The title sounds big. The job is mostly making sure other people can do theirs. I think that is correct. Ask me in a year.
