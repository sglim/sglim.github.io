Four years is long or short depending on who you ask. For me it was long enough to get attached. Today was my last day. I learned a lot and grew a lot here and never wrote any of it down properly, so I am taking the time now.

## The notes

I started tracking my work hours in early 2017. Not commuting, not lunch, just work. There is a gap of about a year in the data but my working pattern did not change much, so I scaled it and got some numbers I find interesting.

Where the time went:

- The travel app, Kyte: a little under half. Almost 48 percent.
- The IoT project, Brilli: 16 percent.
- Company work: 14 percent. All hands, English class, one on ones, culture committee, interviews, the website redo, the issue tracker migration.
- The blockchain task force, Torque: about 8 percent.
- The consumer app, Meerkat: about 7 percent.
- Demo days and hackathon weeks: 5 percent.
- Glatus, a small Flutter and Ktor project: 2 percent.

Kyte was always going to be the biggest. What surprised me is "company work" at 14 percent. One in seven hours was not on any product. I do not think that is bad. That is what it costs to be part of a company instead of just working at one.

Interviews went 6 hours, then 70, then 30, then 3, by year. 2018 was the hiring year. Total is around 109 hours, which at roughly thirty minutes each is something like 150 interviews. Meetings across everything were about 14 percent of all hours. I expected more. It felt like more.

## The things

Hosted the all hands. Sat on the hackathon committee, the offsite committee, the culture committee. Won the internal hackathon week twice and a demo day once. Not every event I touched was a success. But looking back I did a lot of different things the company needed, and the most important ones were about culture, about getting people to look in the same direction. The process was fun and so were the results.

Every performance review I got here was "exceeds expectations", which is the second highest band. I worked hard and I tried to be the person people want on their team. I wish I had gotten to work with more people than I did.

## The end

Four years is not short. Some people have been at one place much longer, but for me this was a real chunk of life. Some of what I wanted here I got. Some I did not. I met genuinely good people and I learned a lot about the actual work.

Leaving is never easy and it should not be. The next time I am about to leave somewhere, I will come back and read this, and hopefully it gives me one useful angle I would not have had otherwise.

Next week I start at the company that is taking Kyte forward. Same product, different building. I will write about that when I understand it.
