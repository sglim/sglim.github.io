It has been about three months since the work stopped. I promised the time tracker would have a number by now. It does. I looked. Here it is, with commentary.

The hours did not drop. That is the headline and it surprised me. December logged more laptop time than most months of actual employment. January is on pace for the same. If you only saw the totals you would think I was busier than ever.

The labels tell the truth. Almost all of that time is unassigned. No project. In seven years of tracking, unassigned time was a rounding error. In these three months it is the majority. The laptop was open. Something was in front. It was not work in the sense the tracker understands.

And the top application changed. For years it was the editor, then the browser, then Slack. This winter the second most used app is YouTube. Not by a little. I knew. Seeing it as a bar next to the editor is different from knowing.

I am not going to be too hard on myself about this. Some of it was genuinely useful. Conference talks, a series on Rust internals I had been meaning to watch, a lot of New York apartment tours. Some of it was the thing you do at eleven at night when you have no standup to log off for. The tracker does not know the difference. I do, mostly.

What I actually did with the months, in order of how much I would defend it:

Job conversations. A lot of them. Coffee, calls, a few real interviews. I relearned how to talk about myself, which I had not done since 2020. The first few were bad. I said "we" about everything and could not explain what I personally did. By the tenth I had a story. Tech lead, CTO, architect, and the through line is that I am the person who reads all the systems and makes them agree. That is a sentence I did not have in November.

The side projects. The crawler runs every morning. The Flutter thing does two things now. It is still not ready to talk about.

The visa. There is a path and I am further along it. That is all I will say publicly.

Being in New York without working. This is the part I did not expect to value. For a year and a half I lived here in a two hump schedule with Seoul in my evenings. These three months I lived here on local time. Museums on a Tuesday. Coffee with people who build things I use. The city is a different place when you are awake for it.

Something is starting in March. I will write about it when I have started. The tracker will get a project label again and I am, honestly, relieved about that. Three months was enough to learn that I do not want to be a person who does not work. I want to be a person who works on the right thing. Those are different, and I needed the gap to see the difference.

Last thing. If you are in this spot, and someone will be, the unassigned hours are not a moral failure. They are data. Look at them once, decide what you want the next three months to look like, and close the tab.
