We launched. Civilization Online is live, published with 2K. I have been in the office for something like sixteen hours and I'm writing this from the couch in the corner because I can't sleep anyway.

I want to write down what launch day actually felt like, because I know in a year I'll remember it as either better or worse than it was.

The morning was quiet. Too quiet. Everything was deployed the night before. We just watched dashboards. Somebody brought donuts. Nobody ate them.

Then the servers opened and the login queue went vertical. This is the part you can't really test. You can run bots, we ran a lot of bots, but bots don't all hit the login button in the same second because they saw a tweet. Real people do. The lobby server held. I say that calmly now. At the time I was refreshing a graph every five seconds like it was going to change if I stared harder.

The world servers were the interesting part. We had planned for a certain number per world. Real players don't spread out the way you think. They all go to the same starting area and they all try to build in the same spot. One world got hot and we watched the tick time creep up. It did not fall over. It got uncomfortable. That's the difference between a good day and a bad day in this job.

The patcher, my boring patcher, worked. People downloaded the day one patch in minutes instead of hours because we were only shipping the diff. Nobody will ever notice that. That's fine. The best infrastructure is the kind nobody notices.

Around 8pm there was a bug. I won't go into it. It was in a system I didn't own but I had touched, and for about twenty minutes I thought it was mine. It was not mine. The relief was physical. Then I felt bad for feeling relieved because it was still somebody's.

Things I learned:

- Stress tests tell you where it breaks, not how it feels when it breaks.
- Have one person who is allowed to say "we are rolling back". Not a committee.
- Eat the donut.

I have been in this industry a while now, big enterprise software, an internship at a search company, and this is the first time I shipped something that people were literally waiting outside the door for. It is a different kind of pressure. I'm not sure I want it every year. But I wanted it once.

Going to try to sleep now. The next patch is on Tuesday.
