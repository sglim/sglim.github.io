I'm writing an end of year post again because last year's turned out to be useful. Not because writing it changed anything by itself, but because a written promise sits in the back of your head and quietly pushes.

One thing I have come to accept this year: you cannot live on efficiency alone. You have to sleep. You have to rest. This is not good or bad, it is just a requirement for being efficient at all. So I am sorting the year into three piles. Growth, rest, and regret.

## Growth

Flutter. About a year in, three internal projects, something like forty thousand lines of Dart across them, and I would guess half of that is mine. It is not an easy framework to bet on, and I hope it makes it, and I think it will. Hot reload plus a UI that is just readable code makes maintenance easy and makes the team fast. Compared to writing Swift and Android UI separately, I would say we are two and a half times faster. That number is a feeling, not a measurement, but it is a strong feeling.

The last project this year is a consumer app, Flutter on the front and TypeScript on the back. The one before that was Flutter with a Kotlin server. Two very different backends, same client stack, and the client stack held up both times. That is the review I promised myself in January.

Hacker News. Started reading it daily. Started for English, kept it for everything else. I wrote about this in October so I will not repeat it.

## Rest

The 3D printer. It arrived in July and I have printed a lot. Nozzle changes are routine now. I replaced the mainboard and the extruder, swapped the cooling fan and duct, then went back to the stock duct because the upgrade was worse. That is the hobby in one sentence. I am not modeling my own parts yet and at this price point it stays a hobby, which is what I want from it.

League of Legends. I went back to it after years. The game got faster, matches end in thirty or forty minutes, sometimes twenty. I am playing champions I never touched before and it is fun. The goal is moderation, and the game gives you statistics, so next year I can write down whether I kept to that.

## Regret

A designer I really respected left the company this year. When I heard the reason, it was close to things I had thought myself a while ago, and I felt both understanding and a little ashamed. Not resentful, just sorry to see it. I think we will run into each other again somewhere.

Looking at the list, this year was better than last year in most ways and more organized in all of them. I am not writing next year's goals here. But writing this down will make next December easier, and that is enough to close the year on.
